Engineering a canoe
Rethinking concrete

Materials, structure, fabrication, and performance—developed together, one season at a time.

A small boat
A complete engineering challenge

Concrete has to do more than float. Our team studies the mix, hull, and construction process together, then puts the result to the test at competition. Chrysalis, our 2026 canoe, brought that work together through a carbon-negative mix, a segmental modular mold, and a meshless design.

Segmented modular mold

Chrysalis used a segmental modular mold: a form assembled from separate sections rather than treated as one continuous piece.

This brings hull design, digital fabrication, and assembly planning into the same process. The formwork has to translate the design into a surface the team can actually cast and finish.

Modular formwork · Concept diagram

Meshless construction

A meshless canoe asks the concrete system and shell to work together without a conventional reinforcing mesh.

It makes mix design and structural behavior closely linked. Our team brings material testing, hull analysis, and construction decisions together rather than solving them in isolation.

Shell construction · Concept diagram

Carbon-negative concrete

The 2026 mix incorporated carbon-sequestering recycled concrete fines. Material selection became part of the canoe’s environmental design, alongside the need for a lightweight, workable concrete.

Developing the mix connects sustainability with the practical demands of casting, finishing, and racing a concrete hull.
